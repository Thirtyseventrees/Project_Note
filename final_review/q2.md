# Exercise 2 - Unsteady Stokes Problem

## Problem Statement
$$\begin{cases}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} - \mu \Delta \mathbf{u} + \nabla p = \mathbf{0} & \text{in } \Omega, t \in (0, T) \\
\text{div } \mathbf{u} = 0 & \text{in } \Omega, t \in (0, T) \\
\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} & \text{on } \Gamma_D, t \in (0, T) \\
\mu \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial \mathbf{n}} - p\mathbf{n} = \boldsymbol{\psi} & \text{on } \Gamma_N, t \in (0, T) \\
\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} & \text{in } \Omega, t = 0
\end{cases}$$

where $\mu > 0$, $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ depends on $\mathbf{x}$ and $t$, $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, $\Gamma_D \cap \Gamma_N = \emptyset$, and $T > 0$.

---

## 2.1 [3 pt] (*) Write the weak formulation of (2)

Let:
$$V = \{\mathbf{v} \in [H^1(\Omega)]^2 : \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \text{ on } \Gamma_D\}$$
$$Q = L^2(\Omega)$$

The weak formulation reads: For all $t \in (0, T)$, find $\mathbf{u}(t) \in V$ and $p(t) \in Q$ such that for all $\mathbf{v} \in V$ and $q \in Q$:

$$\int_\Omega \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} \cdot \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} + \mu \int_\Omega \nabla \mathbf{u} : \nabla \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} - \int_\Omega p \, \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} = \int_{\Gamma_N} \boldsymbol{\psi} \cdot \mathbf{v} \, d\sigma$$

$$\int_\Omega q \, \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} \, d\mathbf{x} = 0$$

with initial condition $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}, 0) = \mathbf{0}$.

The Neumann boundary condition $\mu \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial \mathbf{n}} - p\mathbf{n} = \boldsymbol{\psi}$ is naturally incorporated through integration by parts.

---

## 2.2 [1 pt] Write the spatial approximation of (2) using the Taylor-Hood finite element spaces of degree 3 for the velocity and 2 for the pressure

Let $V_h \subset V$ and $Q_h \subset Q$ be Taylor-Hood finite element spaces:
- Velocity space: $V_h = [X_h^3(\Omega)]^2 \cap V$ (piecewise cubic polynomials, P3)
- Pressure space: $Q_h = X_h^2(\Omega) \cap Q$ (piecewise quadratic polynomials, P2)

Let $\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\}_{i=1}^{N_u}$ be the basis functions for velocity and $\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^{N_p}$ for pressure.

The semi-discrete problem: Find $\mathbf{u}_h(t) \in V_h$ and $p_h(t) \in Q_h$ such that:

$$M \frac{d\mathbf{U}}{dt} + A \mathbf{U} + B^T \mathbf{P} = \mathbf{F}$$

$$B \mathbf{U} = \mathbf{0}$$

where:
- $M_{ij} = \int_\Omega \boldsymbol{\phi}_i \cdot \boldsymbol{\phi}_j \, d\mathbf{x}$ (velocity mass matrix)
- $A_{ij} = \mu \int_\Omega \nabla \boldsymbol{\phi}_i : \nabla \boldsymbol{\phi}_j \, d\mathbf{x}$ (velocity stiffness matrix)
- $B_{ij} = -\int_\Omega \psi_i \, \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\phi}_j \, d\mathbf{x}$ (divergence matrix)
- $F_i = \int_{\Gamma_N} \boldsymbol{\psi} \cdot \boldsymbol{\phi}_i \, d\sigma$ (Neumann boundary term)

## 2.3 [2 pt] Approximate the problem obtained at point 2.2 by the implicit backward Euler method in time. Then explain why the problem obtained at every timestep has a unique solution.

Partition $(0, T]$ into $N_T$ subintervals with $\Delta t = T/N_T$. Let $t^n = n\Delta t$.

### Backward Euler discretization:

$$M \frac{\mathbf{U}^{n+1} - \mathbf{U}^n}{\Delta t} + A \mathbf{U}^{n+1} + B^T \mathbf{P}^{n+1} = \mathbf{F}^{n+1}$$

$$B \mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \mathbf{0}$$

Rearranging:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{\Delta t} M + A & B^T \\ B & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{U}^{n+1} \\ \mathbf{P}^{n+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{\Delta t} M \mathbf{U}^n + \mathbf{F}^{n+1} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$$

### Existence and uniqueness at every timestep:

The system at each timestep is a **saddle point problem**. Existence and uniqueness are guaranteed by verifying:

1. **Coercivity on the kernel**: The matrix $\frac{1}{\Delta t} M + A$ is symmetric positive definite on $\ker(B)$:
   - For $\mathbf{v} \in \ker(B)$: $\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t} M + A\right) \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\|v\|_{L^2}^2 + \mu\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2 > 0$

2. **Inf-sup condition**: Taylor-Hood P3/P2 elements satisfy the inf-sup (LBB) condition:
   $$\inf_{q_h \in Q_h} \sup_{\mathbf{v}_h \in V_h} \frac{\int_\Omega q_h \, \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}_h \, d\mathbf{x}}{\|q_h\|_{L^2} \|\mathbf{v}_h\|_{H^1}} \geq \beta > 0$$

By the theory of saddle point problems (Brezzi's theorem), these two conditions guarantee existence and uniqueness.

## 2.4 [2 pt] Write the expected error estimate for the problem obtained in point 2.3 in terms of both $\Delta t$ and $h$

For Taylor-Hood P3/P2 elements with backward Euler time discretization:

### Velocity error:

$$\|\mathbf{u}(T) - \mathbf{u}_h^{N_T}\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq C \left( \Delta t + h^4 \right)$$

$$\|\mathbf{u}(T) - \mathbf{u}_h^{N_T}\|_{H^1(\Omega)} \leq C \left( \Delta t + h^3 \right)$$

### Pressure error:

$$\|p(T) - p_h^{N_T}\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq C \left( \Delta t + h^3 \right)$$

### Explanation:

- **Time discretization error**: Backward Euler is first-order accurate, giving $O(\Delta t)$
- **Spatial discretization error**:
  - Velocity (P3): $L^2$ error is $O(h^{3+1}) = O(h^4)$, $H^1$ error is $O(h^3)$
  - Pressure (P2): $L^2$ error is $O(h^{2+1}) = O(h^3)$

The total error is the sum of temporal and spatial errors.
