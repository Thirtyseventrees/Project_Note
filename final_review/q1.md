# Exercise 1 - Diffusion-Reaction Problem with Domain Decomposition

## Problem Statement
$$\begin{cases}
-\text{div}(\alpha \nabla u) + \gamma u = f & \text{in } \Omega \\
u = 0 & \text{on } \partial\Omega
\end{cases}$$

where $\alpha > 0$, $\gamma \geq 0$ are constants, $f$ is a given function, and $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$.

---

## 1.1 [1 pt] Write the weak formulation of problem (1)

Let $V = H^1_0(\Omega) = \{v \in H^1(\Omega) : v = 0 \text{ on } \partial\Omega\}$.

The weak formulation reads: Find $u \in V$ such that for all $v \in V$:

$$a(u, v) = F(v)$$

where:
$$a(u, v) = \int_\Omega \alpha \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \, d\mathbf{x} + \int_\Omega \gamma u v \, d\mathbf{x}$$

$$F(v) = \int_\Omega f v \, d\mathbf{x}$$

---

## 1.2 [2 pt] Prove existence and uniqueness of the solution of the problem obtained at point 1.1

We apply the **Lax-Milgram theorem**. We need to verify:

### 1. Continuity of $a(\cdot, \cdot)$:

$$|a(u,v)| = \left|\int_\Omega \alpha \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \, d\mathbf{x} + \int_\Omega \gamma u v \, d\mathbf{x}\right|$$

$$\leq \alpha \|\nabla u\|_{L^2} \|\nabla v\|_{L^2} + \gamma \|u\|_{L^2} \|v\|_{L^2}$$

$$\leq \max(\alpha, \gamma) \|u\|_{H^1} \|v\|_{H^1}$$

So $a(\cdot, \cdot)$ is continuous with constant $M = \max(\alpha, \gamma)$.

### 2. Coercivity of $a(\cdot, \cdot)$:

$$a(v,v) = \int_\Omega \alpha |\nabla v|^2 \, d\mathbf{x} + \int_\Omega \gamma v^2 \, d\mathbf{x}$$

$$\geq \alpha \|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2 + \gamma \|v\|_{L^2}^2$$

**Case 1**: If $\gamma > 0$:
$$a(v,v) \geq \min(\alpha, \gamma) \|v\|_{H^1}^2$$

**Case 2**: If $\gamma = 0$: By the Poincaré inequality, $\|v\|_{L^2} \leq C_P \|\nabla v\|_{L^2}$ for $v \in H^1_0(\Omega)$, so:
$$a(v,v) = \alpha \|\nabla v\|_{L^2}^2 \geq \frac{\alpha}{1 + C_P^2} \|v\|_{H^1}^2$$

In both cases, $a(\cdot, \cdot)$ is coercive.

### 3. Continuity of $F(\cdot)$:

$$|F(v)| = \left|\int_\Omega f v \, d\mathbf{x}\right| \leq \|f\|_{L^2} \|v\|_{L^2} \leq \|f\|_{L^2} \|v\|_{H^1}$$

By the **Lax-Milgram theorem**, there exists a unique solution $u \in V$.

## 1.3 [2 pt] Approximate (1) by the finite element method, using polynomials of degree 2 and provide the corresponding stability and error estimate

Let $V_h = X_h^2(\Omega) \cap H^1_0(\Omega)$ be the finite element space of piecewise quadratic (P2) polynomials.

The discrete problem reads: Find $u_h \in V_h$ such that for all $v_h \in V_h$:

$$a(u_h, v_h) = F(v_h)$$

### Stability estimate (Céa's lemma):

Since $a(\cdot, \cdot)$ is coercive with constant $\alpha_0$ and continuous with constant $M$:

$$\|u - u_h\|_{H^1} \leq \frac{M}{\alpha_0} \inf_{v_h \in V_h} \|u - v_h\|_{H^1}$$

### Error estimates for P2 elements:

Assuming $u \in H^{r+1}(\Omega)$ with $r = 2$:

**$H^1$ error**:
$$\|u - u_h\|_{H^1(\Omega)} \leq C h^2 |u|_{H^3(\Omega)}$$

**$L^2$ error** (by Aubin-Nitsche duality argument):
$$\|u - u_h\|_{L^2(\Omega)} \leq C h^3 |u|_{H^3(\Omega)}$$

## 1.4 [2 pt] (*) Partition the domain $\Omega$ into two disjoint subdomains $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ and write one iteration of the Dirichlet-Neumann method. Is this method convergent?

Let $\Omega = \Omega_1 \cup \Omega_2$ with interface $\Gamma = \partial\Omega_1 \cap \partial\Omega_2$.

### One iteration of the Dirichlet-Neumann method:

Given $u_2^{(k)}$ on $\Gamma$, compute:

**Step 1 (Dirichlet problem on $\Omega_1$)**: Find $u_1^{(k+1)}$ such that:
$$\begin{cases}
-\text{div}(\alpha \nabla u_1^{(k+1)}) + \gamma u_1^{(k+1)} = f & \text{in } \Omega_1 \\
u_1^{(k+1)} = 0 & \text{on } \partial\Omega_1 \cap \partial\Omega \\
u_1^{(k+1)} = u_2^{(k)} & \text{on } \Gamma
\end{cases}$$

**Step 2 (Neumann problem on $\Omega_2$)**: Find $u_2^{(k+1)}$ such that:
$$\begin{cases}
-\text{div}(\alpha \nabla u_2^{(k+1)}) + \gamma u_2^{(k+1)} = f & \text{in } \Omega_2 \\
u_2^{(k+1)} = 0 & \text{on } \partial\Omega_2 \cap \partial\Omega \\
\alpha \frac{\partial u_2^{(k+1)}}{\partial n} = \alpha \frac{\partial u_1^{(k+1)}}{\partial n} & \text{on } \Gamma
\end{cases}$$

**Step 3 (Relaxation)**:
$$u_2^{(k+1)} \leftarrow \lambda u_2^{(k+1)} + (1 - \lambda) u_2^{(k)}$$

### Convergence:

The Dirichlet-Neumann method is **convergent** for elliptic problems, but the convergence rate depends on the relaxation parameter $\lambda$ and the geometry of the subdomains.

- Without relaxation ($\lambda = 1$), the method may diverge or converge slowly
- With appropriate relaxation ($0 < \lambda < 1$), the method converges
- The optimal relaxation parameter depends on the problem and domain decomposition
- For symmetric decompositions, $\lambda \approx 0.5$ often works well
- Convergence is guaranteed for sufficiently small $\lambda$

---

## 1.6 [2 pt] (*) Analysis of relaxation parameter

Setting the maximum number of Dirichlet-Neumann iterations to 15:

**For $\lambda = 1.0$ (no relaxation)**:
- The method may not converge or converge very slowly
- Large oscillations in the solution increment between iterations
- The solution at iteration 15 may still be far from the true solution

**For $\lambda = 0.25$**:
- The method converges more smoothly
- The solution increment decreases monotonically
- Better approximation of the true solution at iteration 15

**Physical interpretation**: The relaxation parameter dampens the information exchange between subdomains, preventing overshooting and ensuring stable convergence.
